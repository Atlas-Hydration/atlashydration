How to Hydrate Before, During, and After Exercise

By Atlas Hydration Research • Part 10 of 12 • 6 min read

Most athletes obsess over how much to drink. But the real game-changer is when you drink it. Hydration timing can be the difference between a personal best and a DNF — between feeling energized at mile 20 and hitting the wall at mile 12.

Research consistently shows that a structured hydration protocol outperforms drinking to thirst alone, especially in hot conditions and events lasting longer than 60 minutes. Here's exactly what the science says about when to hydrate.

Why Timing Matters More Than Volume

Drinking a liter of water right before you train doesn't hydrate you — it just makes you need to find a bathroom. Your body can only absorb about 200-300ml of fluid every 15 minutes. Anything beyond that sits in your stomach, causing bloating, sloshing, and GI distress.

The key insight from exercise physiology is that hydration is a time-dependent process. Your cells need time to absorb water and electrolytes. The sodium-glucose co-transport mechanism that drives fluid absorption operates at a fixed rate — you can't rush it.

That's why the athletes who perform best aren't the ones who drink the most. They're the ones who drink the right amount at the right time.

The Pre-Loading Strategy

Pre-hydration is arguably the most overlooked phase of any training plan. Starting exercise in a dehydrated state — which studies suggest up to 50% of athletes do — means you're already operating at a deficit before the first drop of sweat.

The goal of pre-loading is to ensure your blood plasma volume is topped off, your electrolyte levels are balanced, and your body has a buffer to work with. The American College of Sports Medicine recommends consuming 5-7ml per kg of body weight at least 4 hours before exercise.

For a 70kg athlete, that's roughly 350-500ml — or about one Atlas stick mixed into water, sipped over the course of two hours. The electrolytes in Atlas ensure that fluid is retained rather than flushed straight through your kidneys.

During Exercise: The Replacement Window

Once you start sweating, the clock is ticking. Every 15-20 minutes without fluid intake puts you deeper into a hydration deficit that becomes exponentially harder to recover from mid-session.

The optimal during-exercise strategy is 150-250ml of electrolyte drink every 15-20 minutes. This matches your body's absorption rate and prevents both dehydration and the dangerous over-hydration known as hyponatremia.

"The athletes who bonk at mile 20 didn't fail to hydrate — they failed to hydrate on a schedule. By the time thirst signals fire, you're already 1-2% dehydrated and performance has dropped measurably." — Journal of Sports Sciences, 2024

The Recovery Window

The 30-60 minutes after exercise is your body's most receptive period for rehydration. Blood flow to the gut increases, absorption rates peak, and your cells are primed to pull in fluid and electrolytes.

The science is clear: you need to replace 150% of your sweat losses to fully restore hydration status. Why more than 100%? Because your kidneys continue producing urine even as you rehydrate. The extra 50% accounts for ongoing losses.

Weigh yourself before and after exercise. For every kilogram lost, drink 1.5 liters of electrolyte-rich fluid over the following 2-4 hours. One Atlas stick in 500ml of water immediately post-exercise, paired with a protein-rich meal, is the optimal recovery combination.

Daily Hydration Beyond Exercise

Your hydration strategy shouldn't start and stop with workouts. Chronic mild dehydration — the kind most people live with daily — undermines training adaptations, slows recovery, and impairs sleep quality.

Aim for a baseline of 35-40ml per kg of body weight throughout the day, not counting exercise losses. For a 70kg person, that's about 2.5-2.8 liters. Add electrolytes to at least one of those servings — especially first thing in the morning, when you wake up with 6-8 hours of insensible water loss behind you.

The athletes who see the biggest performance gains aren't just hydrating around workouts. They're treating hydration as a 24/7 training variable — because it is.
